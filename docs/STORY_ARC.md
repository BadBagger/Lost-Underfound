# Lost & Underfound — Story Arc

The three-act throughline. `docs/GAME_CONCEPT.md` has the pitch and cast in brief;
this document is the answer to the question that pitch leaves open — **what is
actually going on in Underneath, and how does Act 1 tie into it.**

Everything here is written to pay off hooks that already exist in
`script/ACT_01_SCRIPT.json`, without changing any of Act 1's recorded lines. Act 1
is fully voiced (49/49 lines) — that's real production cost already spent, so this
arc was built to plug into what's already there, not require reshoots. Where Act 1
plants something, this doc names it explicitly.

## The central mystery

Underneath runs on a currency: buttons. Not metaphorically — an actual small
bureaucracy has grown up around them, self-important and process-obsessed in
exactly the Impossible Complaints register (mundane procedure, deadly serious
people, none of it actually load-bearing). That bureaucracy is **the Button Court**,
and Act 2's title — "The Button Sovereignty" — is literally its name for itself,
not just a theme.

The Court has a problem it doesn't talk about: marbles. Round, shiny, genuinely
valuable, and not something the Court prints or controls. If word got out that
anyone could just find a marble and be instantly richer than a lifetime of
button-filing, the whole button economy — and the Court's authority over it —
stops mattering. So for years, quietly, the Court has been intercepting marbles
that roll into Underneath and filing them away as "unclaimed round assets" before
they ever reach the *official* Lost & Underfound that Bramble runs. Bramble's cubby
wall is real, and it's real lost property — but it's the small, legitimate
front-of-house. The Court's actual holding site is bigger, hidden, and never
audited.

Pip's marble went into that pipeline. It's not lost in the ordinary sense — it was
taken.

## Where Act 1's hooks point

| Act 1 hook | Line(s) | What it actually is |
|---|---|---|
| The Sign-In Log | `act01-011` — "Somebody signed in carrying something 'round and shiny.'" | Not flavor. That's a Court intake courier logging a marble pickup — the same pipeline Pip's marble went through. Someone signed the visitor log because even a secret confiscation still has to go through *a* form; the Court is bureaucratic to its own detriment. This is a real clue Act 2 follows. |
| Bramble's deflection | `act01-019`–`021` — "Very popular around here, actually." / "...popular how?" / "Nothing! Nothing to worry about!" | Bramble half-knows. She's seen marbles pass through and never resurface, has noticed the pattern, and has chosen not to look directly at it because looking at it would mean doing something about it, and Bramble's whole identity is "the one who follows the process." Her Act 2/3 arc is closing the gap between *noticing* and *acting*. |
| Old Bottlecap's age | `act01-035` — "Predates the filing system. Predates me, probably. Predates the couch." | Literal, not just a grumpy exaggeration. Old Bottlecap was Underneath's first authority, before Bramble's filing system and before the Court existed. He stepped back (or was stepped over) when the Court formed around the button standard he originally set up in good faith, and took the toll-gate post instead — a job with no politics, just a clear rule he can actually stand behind. |
| Scuttle's cameo | `act01-015` — "'Scuse! Coming through! Delivery! No time, no time—" | Scuttle is a Court courier. That exact dash-through in Act 1 is Scuttle mid-delivery of an intake parcel — plausibly the very manifest that logged Pip's marble. He doesn't know what he carries; he's never once stopped moving long enough to ask. |

Nothing above requires touching Act 1's script or audio. It's all read as ordinary
color on a first pass and re-reads as plot on a second one — which is the same
trick `department-impossible-complaints` uses.

## The antagonist

**Chairman Toggle**, presiding officer of the Button Court — an ornate, fussy coat
toggle who is not evil, just catastrophically committed to a system he's convinced
himself is for everyone's own good. He is a Humongous-style villain: pompous,
sincere, genuinely funny, defeated by procedure and embarrassment rather than
force. His authority is entirely self-appointed and entirely unquestioned, right up
until it's questioned once.

**The Annex** is where confiscated round objects go — the Court's shadow version of
Bramble's cubby wall, much bigger, unlabeled, unaudited. Grommet guards its
entrance. Nobody has ever asked Grommet what's actually inside, including Grommet.

## Character arcs

- **Pip** — arrives wanting one specific marble back and leaves having accidentally
  dismantled a small unjust institution, mostly by being too new here to know you're
  not supposed to ask "wait, why though?"
- **Bramble** — from complicit-by-omission to the person who actually reforms the
  Lost & Underfound into something the Annex answers to, not the other way around.
  Her closing beat (per `docs/GAME_CONCEPT.md`'s "small goodbye beat with Bramble
  and Grommet") is her finally getting the job she's been pretending she already
  had.
- **Old Bottlecap** — reluctant founder, estranged from the system he started.
  Revealed through the Act 2 red herring (below), not through him volunteering it —
  he doesn't talk about himself, so Pip has to earn it. Ends Act 3 by reopening the
  Grate one more time on his own authority, the first thing he's done as founder
  rather than gatekeeper in years.
- **Grommet** — loyal to whoever's in front of him, never questioned who assigned
  him the Annex or why. His arc is the simplest and most important one emotionally:
  once he understands what he's actually been guarding, his loyalty doesn't
  vanish, it just redirects — to Pip and Bramble, and by Act 3 he's the reason
  anyone gets out at all.
- **Scuttle** — courier who's never once stopped moving long enough to look at what
  he's carrying. Act 2 is the first time he stops. Act 3 is what he does with that.
- **Chairman Toggle** — never redeemed into a friend, but not crushed either;
  Humongous villains lose face, not everything. His comeuppance is procedural and a
  little humiliating, not violent.

## Act 2 — "The Button Sovereignty" (~25 min)

Pip follows the sign-in log's clue into the Court's territory: intake windows,
currency-exchange counters, a public gallery where citizens file button disputes
with total sincerity. Reconnects with Scuttle mid-delivery and, this time, gets him
to actually stop. Meets Grommet at the Annex threshold — too big, too gentle,
completely unmovable by force, so the only route through is trust, not a fight (no
puzzle in this game is ever solved by combat — see `docs/ANIMATION_BIBLE.md`).

**Red herring:** early rumor among Court clerks is that Old Bottlecap is the one
skimming marbles — he's ancient, secretive, guards a gate, motive writes itself.
Pip and Bramble chase it down and it's wrong; what they find instead is Old
Bottlecap's real history as founder, and *that's* what actually explains the
Court's authority (he set the original button standard before the Court took it
and ran).

Ends on Pip and Bramble getting turned away from the Annex by Chairman Toggle
himself, citing correct, real, deliberately-obstructive procedure — "unclaimed
property is unclaimed property, that is the entire point of a filing system" — and
Grommet, now trusted, being the only way through, which he can't do until the
timeline forces the issue in Act 3.

## Act 3 — "The Roar" (~15–20 min)

Dawn sweep is close; tremors and light-flicker ramp per `docs/GAME_CONCEPT.md`.
Grommet lets Pip and Bramble into the Annex — the marble is there, along with
years of other confiscated shine, a small gut-punch of a room. Chairman Toggle
tries to stop them on procedure one last time; Pip and Bramble beat him with his
own paperwork, not force — Pip's marble was never legally "unclaimed" in the first
place, it has an owner standing right there, and the Court's own intake form says
so if you actually read it (a callback to Bramble teaching the verbs in Act 1:
"look at things, closely" wins the whole game one more time). Toggle's comeuppance
is losing face in front of the citizens he's been lecturing, not being destroyed.

The vacuum hits mid-escape. Grommet, huge and finally allowed to be useful for
something that matters, blocks the draft/debris at a chokepoint so everyone gets
clear — the game's one real "guardian" payoff for his `world_height_units: 2.4`
scale, which up to now has only been a sight gag. Old Bottlecap reopens the Grate
on his own initiative for the exit route. Scuttle, fastest thing in Underneath, is
what actually gets everyone through in time.

Closes on the small goodbye beat with Bramble and Grommet (per `docs/GAME_CONCEPT.md`)
— Bramble now running a Lost & Underfound the Annex answers to, Grommet no longer
guarding a secret, Pip shrinking back up with the marble, gone before dawn.
